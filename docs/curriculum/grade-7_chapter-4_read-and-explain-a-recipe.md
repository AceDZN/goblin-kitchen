## שאלות ותשובות

### מהו Imperative verbs lead the method?

Recipe steps often begin with a base verb. Peel, put, add, stir, and serve are imperatives. The person following the recipe is understood, so the steps usually do not repeat you. 'Add the yogurt' names both an action and its object. A bare verb such as add can be unclear if the ingredient is not identified.

### מהו Order carries meaning?

A recipe is not an unordered collection of verbs. The banana is peeled before its pieces go into the bowl. The yogurt is added before the mixture is stirred. First, next, then, and finally can signal this progression when the same method appears as a paragraph. Changing order can change the result or make a step impossible in the intended sequence.

### מהו An object relative clause identifies an ingredient?

A description can identify a noun through something someone does to it. 'Use the pear that you prepared.' That refers to the pear. You is the subject of prepared; the pear receives that action. The phrase distinguishes the prepared pear from another possible pear. This is an explicit object reference: the example keeps that visible, so the sentence's parts are easier to interpret.

### What does that refer to in 'the pear that you prepared'?

The pear, which is the object of the action prepared.

### Why does a short version mention prepared pieces?

It makes clear that an earlier preparation stage is already complete.

## Reading Recipes and Essential Steps

A recipe explains how a dish is made. Its ingredient list tells what is needed; its method gives actions in an order. A reader needs both sections, because a list of foods alone does not explain how they become the named dish.

## Original recipe: Pear and Banana Bowl

This is an original recipe text. Ingredients: one banana; one pear, washed, cored, and cut into small pieces; four tablespoons of plain yogurt. Method: 1. Start with clean hands, a clean bowl, and a clean spoon. 2. Peel the banana and break it into small pieces. 3. Put the banana and prepared pear pieces in the bowl. 4. Add the yogurt. 5. Stir gently and serve. The list describes a prepared pear, so the method can refer to pear pieces without leaving their form unexplained.

עזרה בעברית

ברשימת המרכיבים חפשו כמויות ותיאורים של מצב המזון, ובהוראות חפשו פעולות לפי המספרים. תיאור האגס ברשימה חשוב כדי להבין באיזו צורה הוא נכנס לקערה.

## illustration

First, peel the banana; next, add the fruit and yogurt; finally, stir gently.

## Recipe vocabulary

ingredient / method

An ingredient is a food used in the dish. The method is the sequence of actions. Yogurt belongs to the ingredient list; add belongs to the method.

peel / core

Peel means remove the outside skin in this context. Core refers to the central part of a fruit such as a pear; cored means that part has been removed.

stir / gently

Stir means move a spoon through food to combine it. Gently means with light movement or force. It describes how the action is done.

tablespoon / serve

A tablespoon is a measuring spoon unit in the ingredient list. Serve means present the food ready to eat.

## Original prose version

'First, place the banana and prepared pear pieces in a clean bowl. Next, add four tablespoons of yogurt. Then stir gently. Finally, serve the fruit bowl.' This shorter version assumes the banana pieces are already prepared. It retains the ingredients, yogurt amount, and main order.

עזרה בעברית

הגרסה הקצרה כתובה כפסקה ומניחה שחלק מההכנה כבר נעשה. השוו אותה למתכון המלא ובדקו אילו כמויות ופעולות נשמרו.

עזרה בעברית

המילה because פירושה ״כי״ או ״מפני ש״. החלק שאחריה מסביר למה מבצעים את הפעולה בדרך מסוימת.

A reason with because

Because introduces an explanation in a recipe note.

'Stir gently because the banana pieces are soft' connects a manner of stirring to a reason. The reader interprets the second part as the explanation for the first. It is not an extra ingredient or a new serving quantity.

The reason helps explain the instruction's wording.

## A useful short explanation preserves essentials

'Fruit and yogurt in a bowl' gives a broad idea but loses the amounts and sequence. 'Banana and prepared pear pieces, then four tablespoons of yogurt; stir gently' keeps more of the method. A summary can be shorter while still carrying the information another reader needs.

## Clean preparation language

The first step names clean hands and clean utensils. This follows the World Health Organization's general food-hygiene guidance to keep clean when handling food. The vocabulary describes the condition of hands and tools before the food is combined.
