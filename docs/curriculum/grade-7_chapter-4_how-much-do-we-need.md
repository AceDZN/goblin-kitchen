## שאלות ותשובות

### How many or how much?

How many asks for a number of countable items. How much asks for an amount of uncountable food. 'How many apples?' can be answered 'Four'. 'How much rice?' can be answered '500 grams'. 'How many bags of rice?' asks about bags, so many fits even though rice itself is uncountable here. The noun being counted is what matters, not merely the presence of a food word.

### מהו A container is not an automatic fixed amount?

Different containers may hold different amounts. Two bags can be larger or smaller than one other bag, depending on their labeled sizes. In the fictional source, two 500-gram bags contain the same mass as one 1-kilogram bag. The unit makes the comparison possible. Counting containers and measuring their contents answer related but different questions.

### Why does 'How many bottles of milk?' use many?

Bottles is the countable noun. Milk names the contents.

### Does some rice tell an exact mass?

No. A measurement such as 500 grams supplies that precision.

## Food Quantities and Containers

Food amounts can be expressed through counted items, containers, or measurements. The noun's meaning determines the suitable quantity words. Two apples counts separate fruit; two bottles of water counts containers while water remains an uncountable material noun.

## Countable foods and units

apples / pears

An apple, two apples; a pear, three pears. These are directly counted objects in the list.

loaf / slice

A loaf of bread names a whole shaped quantity. A slice of bread names a cut piece. The plural of loaf is loaves, a special form.

bottle / bag

A bottle of milk and a bag of rice identify containers. The counted nouns are bottle and bag.

gram / kilogram

These are units of mass. One kilogram equals 1,000 grams. A number and a unit together give a precise quantity.

## Quantity patterns

A few and a little describe small positive quantities. A lot of works with both groups.

Articles introduce and identify

A or an can introduce one countable item. The can refer to an item already identified.

'There is an apple in a bag. The apple is green.' An introduces the apple; the refers back to it. The choice a/an follows the next sound: an apple, a pear. 'A milk' does not normally name an ordinary amount of milk in this list.

A countable unit makes the phrase precise: a bottle of milk.

## Some and any in shop language

'We have some rice' states that an amount is available. 'We don't have any small bags' states that none are available. 'Do you have any pears?' asks about availability. Some can also occur in offers and requests; the meanings of the sentence matter more than a rigid rule that every question uses any.

עזרה בעברית

שימו לב אם המשפט מוסר שיש מזון, שולל את קיומו או שואל אם הוא זמין. לא מתרגמים את some ואת any תמיד באותה מילה; המשמעות נקבעת לפי המשפט.

## Too much and enough

'Too much rice' means more than needed in the stated situation. 'Enough rice' means the amount meets the need. Neither phrase gives a universal portion size. In the dialogue, the customer's own list makes the large bag unsuitable; the text does not set a rule for everyone.
